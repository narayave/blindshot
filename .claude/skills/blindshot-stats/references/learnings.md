# What the numbers are teaching (newest first)

No names here (public repo). Event codes only.

## Resume / LinkedIn lines (keep current)

- Built and ran a no-app "digital disposable camera" for live events: guests scan a QR code, shoot blind on a
  9-shot roll, and the whole party's album develops together at a set time.
- Biggest night so far (diwali, Nov 2026): **161 phones** opened the link, **61 signed up**, **38 shot 462 photos**
  across 101 rolls in one evening, with **0 failed uploads**; peak 148 photos in one hour.
- 5x the previous best: Halloween nights had 12 and 3 sign-ups; a partner's promo post plus a live
  coming-soon page brought **69 phones before it even opened**.
- After the party, **62 phones** opened the album; a public "host's picks" preview converted **24%** of
  signed-out visitors into sign-ins.

## diwali (Sat 7 Nov 2026, partner event, 9 shots/roll, album at 12:33am, shooting to 3am)

**Funnel:** 161 opened the link → 108 saw the sign-up → 61 signed up (38% of visitors, 56% of sign-up viewers)
→ 38 shot (62% activation).

**Lessons**

1. **A link that works before the doors open is worth it.** The coming-soon page (with countdown and share QR) got
   69 phones before 6pm, off one partner post. Halloween had no pre-event page and 12 / 3 sign-ups.
2. **Activation, not reach, is the gap.** 38% of sign-ups never shot. Median shooter took 2.5 photos; the top 3
   took 74% of all photos. A few power users carry volume; the typical guest stops after a couple of frames.
3. **Winding the film confused people.** 17 phones pressed SHOOT on an unwound camera (115 blocked presses), mostly
   iPhones, the day the "tap the wheel" hint was trimmed. iPhone shooters averaged 4.7 photos; Android 45
   (power users included). UX lesson: an instruction that disappears costs real shots.
4. **The camera permission prompt is a cliff.** 21 phones denied camera access and 13 never got a camera within
   8 s. That's ~1 in 3 of phones that reached the camera. The fallback (shoot with the phone's camera app) exists
   but people don't find it.
5. **The reveal has to happen while people are still there.** Opening the album at 12:33am, after most had left,
   meant only 5 phones saw it on the night; 59 phones came the next day, mostly within an hour of the host's story.
   Only 26% of guests from the night came back to the album. The "look what I got" moment needs an earlier reveal.
6. **The long tail is short and post-driven.** New album viewers: 59 on day 0, 3 on day 1. Views spike when someone
   posts (19 phones in the hour after the host's story), so the follow-up post is the lever, not time.
7. **Instagram's in-app browser is a separate world.** 12 of 66 sign-ups happened inside it; links tapped in
   Instagram don't carry a Safari sign-in. Hence the view-only "Were you there?" sign-in on the album.
8. **People want photos of themselves, taken by others.** A guest asked to save photos others took of them; the
   album now lets anyone signed in save. Tracking saves (from 9 Nov) will show how common it is.

**Bugs the data found:** the camera started off-screen on 17 phones (returning to the tab on the wrap screen).

**Still unknown (tracking added 9 Nov):** which photos people open and save, how many saves are of others' photos,
Mine vs Everyone use.

## halloween / halloween-fri (30-31 Oct 2026)

Sign-ups and photos only (no visit tracking yet). Fri: 12 signed up, 8 shot, 147 photos (top 3 shot 86%).
Sat (rooftop venue, ~1.4k RSVPs): 3 signed up, 2 shot, 90 photos. Lesson: an RSVP list isn't reach; nobody
pitched the camera to the room.
